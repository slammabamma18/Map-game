Cradle of Nations

A real-world map with real geography: rivers, mountain ranges, deserts, fertile plains, and the mineral deposits that shaped history. Drop people anywhere and watch them clear fields, cut timber, dig mines, raise buildings, and send expeditions out to find what they lack.

  • Settlers places a band of people. A riverbank or coast is the best start; barren camps will pack up and move.
  • Zoom in close to watch crews raise new buildings, farmers and miners at work, windmills turn, derricks nod, fishing boats and coastal traders ply the harbours, and warships moored at naval bases. Fields turn with the seasons; villages stay small while great cities sprawl and rise.
  • From the Renaissance on, nations with a navy and a full treasury charter colonies across the oceans when fertile land or dear minerals make the voyage pay. The Suez and Panama canals are cut in the industrial and modern ages.
  • Eras need minerals in reach: copper for bronze, iron for iron, coal for industry, oil for the modern age. Towns without them send expeditions to find them, or trade for them slowly.
  • Nations form in the Bronze Age and grow through colonies and conquest. Kindred cultures ally; rivals over borderlands and minerals go to war. Conquered peoples of another culture grow restless and rebel.
  • Rulers come and go with their own traits; governments turn from tribes to kings to republics, dictatorships and revolutions. Faiths are founded, spread, convert and split. Plagues break out in crowded cities and travel the roads and sea lanes.
  • Nations trade on a world market where scarce goods cost more. Carts, trains and trucks run the roads and traders sail between ports; open a nation to see its trade routes, or switch on Trade to see them all. Blockading fleets choke ports and shield coasts. Modern great powers build the bomb, and a nuclear strike leaves land poisoned for generations.
  • Open any nation and tap Rule this nation to govern it yourself: declare wars, make peace, form alliances, fund the economy, army, navy, scholars or colonies. The Timeline replays how the map got here.
  • Inspect anything. Bless, Plague, Meteor, Enlighten, War and Nuke tip the scales. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom.
How war works

Every nation taxes its GDP (farming, mining, trade) and pays for administration and an army. Armies are capped by what the treasury can afford; a war lets a nation spend down its savings to field more. Each settlement near an enemy border is contested every few years: attackers weigh nearby troops, national military strength and prestige against the defender's garrison, walls, capital status and unrest. Captures bring loot and prestige; losses raise war exhaustion. Exhausted or bankrupt nations sue for peace, truces last fifty years, and a nation that loses half its settlements is conquered outright. Allies join wars, and conquered peoples of another culture rise up when the moment comes. Navies matter too: in peace a squadron patrols between a nation's ports; in war fleets hunt enemy fleets, blockade and bombard ports, prey on the sea lanes that carry an enemy's trade (each raided route cuts its trade income), and from the Renaissance can land marines to seize lightly held ports. Merchantmen sail real sea lanes between trade partners, threading straits and rounding capes, and the Suez and Panama canals shorten them once cut.
